Course Information

Overview and practical application of major developmental concepts from birth to adolescence within different biological, familial, social and cultural contexts.

Across the semester, we will study physical, cognitive, social, and emotional development from conception through adolescence. We will learn the major concepts, theories, and research methods of developmental science and practice applying them to children, adolescents, families, and everyday life.

Course Objectives

  1. Gain an understanding about the major concepts, theories, and methods in the study of physical, cognitive, social, and emotional development from conception through adolescence.
  2. Examine major concepts, theories, and methods related to child development within different biological, familial, racial, cultural, and political contexts.
  3. Apply knowledge about child development to practical applications in multiple settings.

Student Learning Outcomes

At the conclusion of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Describe important developmental outcomes related to physical, cognitive, social, and emotional development from conception through adolescence.
  2. Describe major theories in the field of child development.
  3. Recognize different research methodologies used to study child development.
  4. Discuss socioeconomic, cultural, and racial influences on development within a historical and political context.
  5. Explain the significance of early experiences to later outcomes.
  6. Discuss current trends in the field of child development, such as media use and education.
  7. Analyze real-world child/adolescent/family experiences to identify important developmental outcomes.
  8. Write a reflection on their own experiences using the key concepts discussed in the course.

Overall Learning Objectives for Area 4

After completing courses from different disciplinary perspectives in Area 4, students shall:

  1. Understand the ways that social, political, and economic institutions and human behavior are interconnected.
  2. Understand problems and issues from respective disciplinary perspectives and examine issues in their contemporary as well as historical settings and in a variety of cultural contexts.
  3. Understand the principles, value systems, ethics, and methodologies employed in social science inquiry.
  4. Understand the ways cultures construct social differences, such as those based on ethnicity, gender, race, class, and sexual orientation, and their effects on the individual and society.

Area 4A · Introduction to the Social Sciences

Students completing courses in subarea 4A shall:

  1. Understand the purpose of the social sciences and the distinguishing features of the social sciences.
  2. Understand and explain major social science concepts, methods, and theories and apply them to concrete problems of contemporary society.
  3. Reflect on what it means to be a social, historical, cultural, psychological, and political being.
  4. Reflect on their own social, cultural, and political experiences in light of social science concepts, methods, and theories.
  5. Understand the integrated nature of social, political, and economic behaviors and institutions in different geographical and historical contexts.
  6. Understand processes of social, political, and cultural change and differentiation in a variety of cultural contexts.

Guidelines for Exploration

This course is built around the idea that learning is a shared exploration. These guidelines are meant to make expectations clear while leaving room for curiosity, growth, and the realities of being a person.

Participation & Engagement

Participation looks different for different people. You might think out loud, listen closely, contribute in a small group, ask a question, make a connection, or take time to reflect before speaking. What matters is being present in ways that genuinely support your learning and the learning of the people around you.

Attendance & Missed Class

Our class meetings are where much of the exploration, practice, and application happens, so attendance is strongly encouraged. If illness, transportation, work, family responsibilities, or unexpected circumstances keep you from class, take care of what you need to take care of and reconnect with the course as soon as you can. You are responsible for checking Canvas and the course schedule for anything you missed.

Deadlines, Extensions & Late Work

Deadlines give the semester a workable rhythm, and I want you to communicate early when something is getting in the way. If you need an extension, contact me before the deadline whenever possible; approved extensions will follow the new agreed-upon due date without penalty. Otherwise, late assignments will be reduced by 10% of the assignment’s total points for each calendar day they are late and will not be accepted more than 7 calendar days after the original due date. In-class activities and exams may require a different arrangement because they depend on what happens in class. Contact me as soon as possible if you miss one.

Technology & AI

AI can be a useful thinking partner when it helps you explore ideas, ask better questions, clarify something you do not understand yet, organize your thinking, practice with course material, or get feedback on how clearly you are communicating. The goal is for AI to support your learning, not do the learning for you.

If you use AI for an assignment, briefly say what you used it for and how it helped. Your final work should still reflect your own understanding, judgment, and voice, and you are responsible for checking the accuracy of anything AI gives you.

Using AI to produce work that you submit as your own, generate an entire assignment, or bypass the thinking an assignment is designed to help you practice is not permitted. If you are unsure whether a particular use is appropriate, ask before submitting. Misuse of AI may be treated as an academic integrity issue.

Academic Integrity

Academic integrity means taking responsibility for your work and taking care of the ideas you use. Give credit when you draw on another person’s words, research, media, or ideas. Do not submit another person’s work as your own, fabricate sources or information, or use unauthorized assistance on an exam or assignment. Suspected violations of CSUF’s academic integrity policy will be reported to the Dean of Students. Depending on the nature of the violation, a student may receive a zero on the assignment or exam, and additional university disciplinary action may apply. If you are unsure what is appropriate, ask before submitting.

Feedback and grades will be posted in Canvas. You are also welcome to reach out when you want to talk through a concept, an assignment, or how things are going in the course.

If you are struggling with course content, an assignment, keeping up with the course, or something that is making it difficult to participate, please reach out. You are welcome to come to office hours or email me so we can talk through what is getting in the way and identify a workable next step.

If you encounter an accessibility barrier in course materials or activities, please let me know as soon as possible.

I am committed to fostering a respectful and inclusive environment. Both I and the university are here to support your safety, dignity, and well-being. If you experience or witness discrimination, harassment, or behavior that undermines a safe learning space, there are resources available to support you. I am always willing to help connect you with the support you need.
